# Scorebug styles

Choose a sport first, then a style. The sport controls the score, clock, statistics and animations. The style controls how those details look. For example, NBA games can use Blades while keeping basketball information.

Click the star beside **Scorebug style** to save the current look, or star any card in **Browse styles**. Saved looks appear in **Favorite styles** for quick switching. Turn on **Favorites only** in the browser to see just those cards; search and the **All sports** filter still work. Starring a card saves it without changing your game or active style. Click its name or preview to use it.

These responsive styles are inspired by the 83 score and timing layouts in the Scorebug Reference Atlas. The dates identify the reference era. Rows marked “example” give the date of that example. Scorebug uses its own layouts and licensed open fonts. Normal and Side views both support the full catalogue.

The atlas’s F1 TV Multiview reference (67) is a video-viewing interface. Timing styles 60–66 provide the scorebug portion; the app does not include broadcast video or onboard footage.

## Adapting layouts

- Team logos or their timeout marks open team statistics. The clock opens team comparisons. Racing and golf session headers open classification details, with the complete table in Game center.
- Football shows down, distance and reported field position. Basketball uses period, clock, reported shot clock and bonus. Baseball uses inning, count, outs and reported bases.
- Tennis and volleyball keep reported set and point scores. Soccer and rugby keep their own clock and match status. Racing and golf show a classification rather than pretending a contest has two teams.
- The compact racing view shows the leading drivers; hover its session header or open Game Center for the complete order and available timing.
- Missing feed values stay unavailable. Changing a visual style never creates data that a provider has not supplied.
